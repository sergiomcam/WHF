# Exposure Explorer interaction check

Open Main.unity and enter Play Mode. ExposureManager builds the Explorer inside its existing module panel, cloning the home card's template-derived button and typography. The activity catalog consists of nine editable ScriptableObjects under Assets/_HearingNotion/ScriptableObjects/Activities. No research prevalence or measured sound levels are fabricated.

1. Open Exposure Explorer. Switch Environmental, Recreational, and Occupational categories; each shows three activities. Scroll with the wheel or drag the content on touchscreens.
2. Open an activity and inspect its description and prevention suggestion. Add it to your activities. Open another category, add another activity, and return: the selections and count should persist.
3. Remove an activity and confirm that its card and count update. Repeated add/remove should not create duplicates.
4. Choose Explore this sound. Sound Experience displays that activity's context and states that the audio example is pending. It plays no audio.
5. Return Home and open Personalized Takeaways. It lists the selected activities and their suggestions; scroll to see all entries. An empty session displays an explicit empty state.
6. Reset visitor session from the Explorer or Takeaways. Welcome returns, all activity selections and sound context clear, and a later Takeaways visit is empty.
7. Repeat at portrait and landscape aspect ratios, including changing aspect while the Explorer is open. Scroll areas remain inside the existing responsive panel; navigation stays outside the scroll area.

Compilation and serialized scene-reference checks passed. Unity Play Mode, touch interaction and visual verification have not been performed by the agent. Module content is currently English; bilingual copy remains pending. Selections are stored in memory only and are not an acoustic exposure estimate or a hearing assessment.

Prevention copy follows general principles in WHO safe-listening guidance: https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/deafness-and-hearing-loss-safe-listening . Workplace examples also remind visitors to follow their workplace controls and required protection; this prototype does not prescribe task-specific protective equipment.
