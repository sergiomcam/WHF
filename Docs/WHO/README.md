# WHO noise and safe-listening reference library

Reviewed 2026-11-05. Original downloaded pages/PDFs and retrieval-records.json are in the project's local ReferenceLibrary/WHO folder. The record contains source/resolved URLs, UTC retrieval time, byte size and SHA-256. Seven official sources were downloaded successfully. These are selected references, not an exhaustive systematic review, clinical advice, WHO endorsement or proof of compliance. Original files are excluded from GitHub documentation publication.

| Source | Publication / scope | App relevance and review limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [WHO safe-listening Q&A](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/deafness-and-hearing-loss-safe-listening) | 6 March 2026; public guidance | Relates level to duration: the page gives 80 dB for 40 hours/week versus 90 dB for four hours/week. Encourage lower comfortable volume, breaks and protection; these examples are not individualized guarantees or app output settings |
| [Safe listening devices and systems](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241515276) | WHO–ITU, 2019; device-level information and exposure management | Relevant to education and future calibrated dose features; the prototype does not measure headphone SPL or implement certified dosimetry. Archived publication page, not the complete linked standard PDF |
| [Safe listening venues and events](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240043114) | WHO, 2022; venue practice | Six-feature standard addresses monitoring, acoustics, protection, quiet areas and information. Archive includes the official publication page; full standard download requires separate retrieval/review |
| [Environmental noise guidelines](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789289053563) | WHO Europe, 2018; environmental sources | Keep long-term environmental indicators separate from recreational listening and occupational exposure. Publication page archived; full guideline text is not claimed downloaded |
| [Environmental-noise compendium chapter](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/who-compendium-on-health-and-environment/who_compendium_noise_01042022.pdf) | WHO/UN guidance, 2022 update; eight-page PDF | PDF page 3 lists road-traffic recommendations below 53 dB Lden and below 45 dB Lnight; occasional leisure ≤100 dB LAeq,15min. These use different averaging periods and contexts; never turn 100 dB into a universal safe listening target |
| [Gameplay and esports standard](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240108028) | WHO–ITU, 2025 | Relevant to planned distraction audio: volume controls, safe-listening features and warnings. Publication page archived; app compliance is not established |
| [Gameplay/esports summary](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/documents/health-topics/deafness-and-hearing-loss/summary---safe-listening-for-video-gameplay-and-esports.pdf?sfvrsn=b07d8ffd_3) | Official four-page summary | PDF page 2 describes device/software scope and developer-facing safe-listening features. It is a summary, not a replacement for the complete technical standard |

## Maintenance

Tools/Tests/fetch_who_references.py re-downloads only these official WHO origins and records failures rather than substituting secondary material. Inspect new versions, supporting passages, publication dates and rights before changing app claims. Numeric values above are source-specific reference points, not acoustic measurements from Hearing Notion. Preserve units and averaging periods; the future microphone module remains deferred. Do not embed WHO logos or imply endorsement. See [ETHICS.md](../ETHICS.md) for source-veracity procedures.
